# Local peak growth and finite-horizon Cauchy deformation covariance are non-equivalent NS mechanisms

Status: **Exact two-flow Navier--Stokes calibration / counterexample pair.**

The local peak metric/q.v. theorem and the current Kelvin vectorized Cauchy
covariance law describe different physical questions:

- `D_t e` asks whether vorticity energy is growing **now at the physical point**;
- `Sigma_D(h)` asks how much stochastic Cauchy deformation varies across backward
  anchors over a **finite causal past horizon**.

Two exact Navier--Stokes flows separate these mechanisms in both directions.

---

## 1. Affine vortex: positive peak growth with zero deformation covariance

Take the exact affine vortex-stretch flow

\[
A(t)=
\begin{pmatrix}
-a&-r(t)&0\\
r(t)&-a&0\\
0&0&2a
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
r(t)=r_0e^{2at},
\qquad a>0.
\]

The velocity gradient is spatially uniform.  Therefore every backward stochastic
anchor samples exactly the same `A(t-sigma)`, and the Cauchy deformation `D_h` is
deterministic conditional on the current endpoint.  Hence

\[
\boxed{
\Sigma_D(h)=0,
\qquad
C_D^{\rm Gram}(h)=0
}
\]

for every horizon.

The vorticity is

\[
\omega=(0,0,2r_0e^{2at}),
\]

also spatially uniform, so

\[
\nabla\omega=0,
\qquad
\Delta e=0.
\]

Nevertheless

\[
\boxed{
D_t e
=\omega\cdot S\omega
=8ar_0^2e^{4at}>0.
}
\]

Every point is a non-strict spatial local enstrophy maximum.

**Classification: EXACT NAVIER--STOKES CALIBRATION.**

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\text{positive local peak growth}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{nonzero stochastic deformation covariance}.
}
\]

The growth is pure deterministic/common strain work.

## 2. Periodic one-mode shear: positive deformation covariance at a decaying enstrophy maximum

Take the exact smooth periodic Navier--Stokes shear

\[
u=(E(t)\cos ky,0,0),
\qquad
E(t)=e^{-\nu k^2t}.
\]

Its vorticity is

\[
\omega=(0,0,kE\sin ky).
\]

At

\[
y_*=\frac{\pi}{2k},
\]

enstrophy is a strict spatial maximum in the active `y` direction.  There

\[
\boxed{
\omega\cdot S\omega=0,
\qquad
\nabla\omega=0,
}
\]

while

\[
\Delta e=-k^4E^2<0.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
D_t e
=-\nu k^4E^2<0.
}
\]

The current instantaneous peak is decaying.

Yet the exact stochastic Cauchy deformation is

\[
D_h=I+c_hE_{21}
\]

and the upstream closed moment formula gives at `y_*`, with

\[
\alpha=\nu k^2,
\]

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}(c_h)
=k^2e^{-2\alpha t}
\left[
\frac{\cosh(2\alpha h)-1}{2\alpha^2}
-h^2
\right]>0
}
\]

for every `h>0`, because

\[
\cosh(2x)>1+2x^2
\qquad(x>0).
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
C_D^{\rm Gram}(h)
=\operatorname{Var}(c_h)e_2e_2^T>0,
}
\]

and the full vectorized covariance is nonzero as well.

**Classification: EXACT NAVIER--STOKES / STOCHASTIC CAUCHY CALIBRATION.**

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\text{nonzero finite-horizon deformation covariance}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{positive local peak growth}.
}
\]

## 3. The two clocks/mechanisms are physically different

At the shear maximum the instantaneous Kelvin vorticity-gradient q.v. density is
zero because `grad omega=0`, yet the finite-horizon deformation covariance is
positive.  There is no contradiction:

- instantaneous Kelvin q.v. samples the current vorticity gradient;
- Cauchy deformation covariance is generated by Brownian anchors sampling the
  **spatial variation of velocity gradient** over a past horizon.

Likewise the affine vortex has strong current strain work but no anchor-induced
deformation dispersion because `grad A=0` spatially.

Therefore the exact physical distinction is

\[
\boxed{
\text{current directional stretching}
\quad\neq\quad
\text{finite-horizon deformation dispersion}.
}
\]

**Classification: EXACT TYPE SEPARATION.**

stochastic deformation spread lives in a direction not used by the instantaneous
